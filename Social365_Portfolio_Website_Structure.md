# Social365 --- Portfolio Website Structure & Content Specification

> **Document purpose:** Define the recommended website pages, their
> responsibilities, and the content each page should contain. This is a
> planning document, not final marketing copy.
>
> **Working brand context:** Social365 (Company Name)
>
> **Business positioning:** A digital growth agency focused on strategic
> marketing, creative storytelling, and impactful brand communication.
>
> **Services currently identified:** Social Media Management, Influencer
> Marketing, and Digital Marketing.

---

## 1. Recommended Website Architecture

**Recommendation:** Keep the nine ideas as a starting point, but
organize them into a clearer website architecture. Do not make every
item a separate top-level page automatically. Some are best as sections
on the Home page; others deserve dedicated pages as the content grows.

### Primary navigation

- Home
- Services
- Our Work
- About
- **Let's Talk** (primary contact/lead-generation button)

### Secondary navigation or footer links

- Case Studies (can begin as a section within Our Work, then become a
  dedicated page when enough detailed cases are available)
- Our Process (can be a dedicated page or a strong section on the
  About page)
- Pricing / Packages (publish only if packages and pricing are
  approved; otherwise use "Engagement Options" or "Request a
  Proposal")
- Contact
- Privacy Policy
- Terms of Service

### Final page list for the first version

1.  Home
2.  Services
3.  About
4.  Our Work
5.  Our Process
6.  Pricing / Packages (conditional on approved pricing)
7.  Contact
8.  Privacy Policy
9.  Terms of Service

**Case Studies decision:** Treat case studies as a content type within
**Our Work** at launch. Give each substantial case its own detail page
when there is enough verified information to tell a useful story. This
avoids an empty or repetitive standalone page.

**Why this structure:** It gives prospective clients a clear path from
understanding the agency, to reviewing services and proof, to learning
how the agency works, and finally making an inquiry. It also avoids
cluttering the main navigation with every possible page.

## 2. Page-by-Page Content Specification

## Page 1 --- Home

**Goal:** Explain the agency's value quickly, establish credibility, and
guide visitors to explore services, work, or contact.

### Recommended sections

1.  **Hero section**
    - Clear headline about the business outcome the agency helps
      clients pursue.
    - Short supporting statement explaining the agency's approach.
    - Primary CTA: "Book a Discovery Call" or "Let's Talk".
    - Secondary CTA: "Explore Our Work".
    - Strong brand visual, campaign collage, or relevant creative.
2.  **Trust / proof strip**
    - Verified client logos, industries served, partner logos, or
      approved credentials.
    - If these are not available, omit the strip rather than inventing
      proof.
3.  **Services overview**
    - Brief cards for Social Media Management, Influencer Marketing,
      and Digital Marketing.
    - Each card links to the relevant service detail.
4.  **Selected work**
    - A curated selection of projects or campaigns.
    - Show the client/brand, objective, services delivered, and a
      short outcome where verified.
5.  **Why work with us**
    - Three to five real differentiators, such as strategy-led
      planning, creative execution, collaboration, or
      reporting---confirm which accurately describe the company.
6.  **How we work**
    - A compact overview of the process from discovery through
      delivery and reporting.
    - Link to Our Process.
7.  **Testimonials or client feedback**
    - Use only approved, genuine testimonials with attribution.
    - Hide this section until suitable testimonials are available.
8.  **Final CTA**
    - Invite visitors to discuss their goals or request a proposal.
9.  **Footer**
    - Contact details, social links, key pages, legal links, and
      copyright.

### Home page should not

- Try to explain every service in full.
- Display invented metrics, fake testimonials, or unapproved client
  logos.
- Repeat the full About page or Process page verbatim.

clients identify the service relevant to their needs.

### Intro section

- A concise description of the agency's service philosophy.
- Explain that services can be offered individually or combined, if
  that is genuinely supported.

#### A. Social Media Management

Potential content to confirm: - Social media strategy and content
planning. - Content calendars and publishing. - Creative direction and
content production coordination. - Community management and
engagement. - Platform-specific optimization. - Performance reporting.

#### B. Influencer Marketing

Potential content to confirm: - Campaign strategy and creator
selection. - Influencer outreach and coordination. - Briefing and

reporting and learnings.

#### C. Digital Marketing

This category is broad, so specify the actual services offered before
publishing. Potential areas to confirm include: - Paid advertising. -
Campaign strategy. - Search engine optimization (SEO). - Email

- What the service is.
- The business need it addresses.
- What is included and excluded.
- Typical deliverables.
- How success is measured, where relevant.
- Related work or case studies.
- FAQs.
- CTA to discuss the service.

## Page 4 --- Our Work

**Goal:** Demonstrate the quality and range of the agency's creative and
marketing work.

### Recommended sections

1.  **Introduction**
    - A short statement about the type of work shown.
2.  **Project / campaign gallery**
    - Visual previews of campaigns, content, brand collaborations, or
      other deliverables.
3.  **Filters, if there is enough content**
    - By service, industry, campaign type, or platform.
    - Avoid filters that leave visitors with empty results.
4.  **Project cards**
    - Project or brand name, category, short description, and link to
      details.
5.  **CTA**
    - Invite visitors to discuss a similar challenge.

### For each work item, include where available

- Client or brand name (with permission).
- Project context and objective.
- Services provided.
- Selected visuals or deliverables.
- The agency's role and scope.
- Results only when measurable and approved.
- Link to a detailed case study if one exists.

**If client work is confidential:** Use approved anonymized descriptions
or concept work clearly labeled as such. Never imply that speculative
work was a paid client engagement.

## Page 5 --- Case Studies

**Launch decision:** Case studies should initially live inside Our Work.
A dedicated Case Studies index and individual detail pages are
recommended once there are enough strong, approved examples.

**Goal:** Show how the agency approached a real business problem and
what happened as a result.

### Case study template

1.  **Overview**
    - Client/industry, project period, services, and agency role.
2.  **Challenge**
    - The client's problem or objective.
3.  **Goals**
    - What the project aimed to improve.
4.  **Strategy**
    - Audience insight, positioning, channel selection, and campaign
      plan.
5.  **Execution**
    - Content, creator partnerships, campaigns, or other work
      delivered.
6.  **Results**
    - Verified outcomes with timeframe and context.
7.  **Learnings**
    - What the team learned or would optimize next.
8.  **Related work / services**
9.  **CTA**

Use real figures only when the client has approved them and the
measurement method is understood. If results cannot be shared, describe
the deliverables and approach without fabricating performance claims.

## Page 6 --- Our Process

**Goal:** Set expectations and make working with the agency feel
organized, transparent, and collaborative.

The company's actual process will be supplied separately. Until then,
use this as a **placeholder framework**, not a claim about the agency's
existing operations.

### Suggested process stages

1.  **Initial inquiry**
    - Client shares their business, goals, needs, budget range, and
      timing.
2.  **Discovery conversation**
    - Discuss the audience, challenges, expectations, and fit.
3.  **Research and strategy**
    - Review relevant context and define the recommended direction.
4.  **Proposal and scope**
    - Confirm deliverables, responsibilities, timeline, pricing, and
      success measures.
5.  **Onboarding**
    - Agreements, access, contacts, assets, approvals, and
      communication channels.
6.  **Planning and creation**
    - Build the campaign/content plan and prepare creative for review.
7.  **Review and approval**
    - Client feedback, revisions, and final sign-off under the agreed
      scope.
8.  **Launch / delivery**
    - Publish, activate, or hand over the agreed deliverables.
9.  **Reporting and optimization**
    - Share relevant performance insights and recommended next steps,
      if included in the service.
10. **Ongoing partnership or closeout**

- Agree on the next cycle, renewal, handover, or project completion.

### Explain alongside the stages

- What the client needs to provide.
- What the agency is responsible for.
- How feedback and approvals work.
- Expected communication cadence.
- How revisions, delays, and scope changes are handled.
- What deliverables and reporting the client receives.

Replace this framework with the company's real workflow before launch.

## Page 7 --- Pricing / Packages

**Goal:** Help clients understand the available engagement options and
qualify inquiries.

### First decide which pricing model the company uses

- **Public packages:** Display prices and clearly defined inclusions.
- **Starting prices:** Show a genuine minimum price, with
  scope-dependent details.
- **Custom proposals:** Explain the factors that determine price and
  invite an inquiry.
- **Hybrid:** Publish selected packages and offer custom proposals for
  larger engagements.

### If packages are approved, each package should show

- Package name and best-fit client.
- Included services and deliverables.
- Quantity / frequency (for example, content volume or reporting
  cadence).
- Platforms or channels covered.
- Number of revision rounds, if applicable.
- Client responsibilities and exclusions.
- Contract duration or minimum commitment.
- Price, billing frequency, taxes, and any setup fees where relevant.
- Clear CTA to inquire or book a call.

### If prices are not ready

Use an "Engagement Options" page or a "Request a Proposal" section
instead of fake prices. Explain that the scope is tailored to goals,
deliverables, channels, and campaign requirements.

Do not promise specific business results as part of a package.

## Page 8 --- Contact

**Goal:** Make it easy for a prospective client to start a useful
conversation.

### Recommended sections

1.  **Contact headline**
    - A welcoming invitation to discuss a project or business goal.
2.  **Inquiry form**
    - Name.
    - Work email.
    - Company / brand.
    - Website or social profile (optional).
    - Service of interest.
    - Brief project description or goals.
    - Approximate budget range (optional, if the company uses it).
    - Desired timeline (optional).
    - Consent / privacy notice appropriate to the form.
3.  **Direct contact details**
    - Public business email.
    - Phone / WhatsApp only if approved.
    - Location or business hours if relevant.
4.  **Social links**
5.  **What happens next**
    - A realistic response expectation or next step, once confirmed.

Keep the form focused. Do not request sensitive information that is
unnecessary for an initial business inquiry.

## Page 9 --- Privacy Policy

**Goal:** Explain how the website handles visitors' and prospective
clients' personal data.

The policy must reflect the website's actual practices and applicable
laws. It should cover, where relevant: - What information is collected
through forms, analytics, cookies, and other tools. - Why the
information is collected and the applicable legal basis where
relevant. - How the information is used and shared. - Service providers
or third parties involved. - Data retention and security practices. -
Cookies and analytics choices. - User rights and how to make a privacy
request. - Contact details for privacy questions. - Effective date and
how material updates will be communicated.

**Implementation note:** This specification is not legal advice or a
finished privacy policy. Have the final text reviewed for the company's
jurisdiction, audience, data flows, and actual tools before publishing.

---

## Page 10 --- Terms of Service

**Goal:** Set the rules for using the website and clarify what the
website does and does not promise.

Topics to consider, as applicable: - Acceptance and scope of the
terms. - Permitted use of the website and its content. - Intellectual
property and permitted use of portfolio materials. - Accuracy and
availability of website content. - Inquiry, proposal, and contracting
process. - Third-party links and platforms. - Appropriate limitations of
liability. - Governing law and dispute handling, as reviewed by
counsel. - Updates to the terms. - Contact details and effective date.

Website terms should not be treated as a substitute for a separate
client services agreement covering project scope, payment, deliverables,
revisions, ownership, confidentiality, cancellation, and other
engagement-specific terms.

**Implementation note:** Obtain suitable legal review before publishing.

## 4. Content Rules: What Makes It Feel Professional

1.  **Be specific.** Explain what the agency actually does rather than
    relying only on phrases such as "we deliver excellence."
2.  **Show evidence.** Use real work, approved client names, genuine
    testimonials, and verified results.
3.  **Be transparent.** Clarify deliverables, scope, pricing approach,
    and responsibilities.
4.  **Keep the journey clear.** Each page should help visitors take a
    sensible next step.
5.  **Avoid duplication.** Home previews the services and work; the
    dedicated pages provide details.
6.  **Do not invent credentials.** No fake client logos, testimonials,
    metrics, awards, team members, or case studies.
7.  **Use approved information.** Confirm company name, contact details,
    service list, process, pricing, and permission to publish client
    work.
8.  **Make legal pages accurate.** Policies must match the website's
    real data collection and business practices.

## 6. Final Recommendation

For the initial release, build the following core experience:

**Home → Services → Our Work → About → Our Process → Pricing /
Engagement Options → Contact**

Add **Privacy Policy** and **Terms of Service** as footer-linked legal
pages.

Keep **Case Studies** within Our Work until there is enough approved,
meaningful content for a dedicated index and detailed case pages.
Publish Pricing / Packages only when the company has approved a real
pricing model; otherwise use a proposal-led page.

This structure gives the website the depth and credibility expected of a
professional digital marketing agency while remaining practical, honest,
and easy to navigate.
